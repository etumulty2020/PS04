What the changes felt like on my back

The lumbar pad is the upgrade you'd feel

On the table, the 5.0's lumbar pad looks enormous. On my back, it felt supportive and soon faded into the background, and I had no lower-back discomfort on the filmed hike.

The reason a bigger, contoured pad matters is the curve of your lower back. A pad that fills more of that hollow gives the pack a firmer second point of contact below your shoulder blades, so the bottom of the pack has less room to rock with each step. That second contact probably did as much for the 5.0's stability on rough ground as the plate pocket did.

So the upgrade question mostly comes down to your 4.0's lumbar pad. If you finish a ruck with a sore or rubbed lower back, or you feel the bottom of the pack moving, that's the problem the 5.0 was built to fix. If you never think about your lower back in a 4.0, you already have most of what the 5.0 offers.

The magnetic pockets are a quality-of-life change

I kept my keys in a magnetic internal pocket, and it was simply easy: one hand, no rip of hook-and-loop. Magnets are quiet and don't clog with lint, sand or dog hair the way hook-and-loop does over years of use. They're a genuine quality-of-life upgrade, and if your 4.0's pockets have worn out and stopped holding, they count for even more.

What didn't change

The plate still rides high and tight against your back, which is why both generations carry better than an everyday bag with a plate sleeve. With a 20 lb plate, the 5.0 stayed stable where I had to work to keep my own balance. Strap setup, the sternum strap and the optional hip belt work the same way in both generations, and my full review goes into what I'd change for heavier loads.

Should you upgrade from a Rucker 4.0?

Upgrade to the 5.0 if:

Your lower back gets sore, rubbed or hot in the 4.0, or you feel the bottom of the pack rocking.

The hook-and-loop on your 4.0's internal pockets has worn out and no longer holds.

You're moving up to heavier plates, where lumbar contact and stability matter more.

You've been meaning to change sizes anyway.

Keep your 4.0 if:

It fits well and you finish rucks without lower-back complaints.

Most of your frustration is shoulder soreness. The lumbar pad won't fix that; a snug sternum strap, a hip belt or building up the weight more gradually will do more. Our rucking form guide covers strap setup.

You'd rather put the money toward a heavier plate or better shoes.

GORUCK hasn't announced a way to retrofit the new lumbar pad or magnetic closures to a 4.0, so upgrading means replacing the bag.
