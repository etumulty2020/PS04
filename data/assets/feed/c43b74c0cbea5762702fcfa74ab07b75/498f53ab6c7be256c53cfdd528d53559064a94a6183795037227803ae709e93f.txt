Our catalog picks, and which were discounted

Below are the GORUCK items from our catalog that a Spring Sweat shopper was most likely comparing. Only one of the three was part of the sale.

The Rucker 4.0 was not discounted. Archived copies of GORUCK's deals page show it at full price during the sale, while the lower-cost Basic Rucker took the deepest markdown. The 4.0 is GORUCK's previous-generation dedicated rucking pack, with dual elevated plate pockets, a frame sheet, a 1000D CORDURA shell and the SCARS Lifetime Guarantee; a hip belt is an optional extra. It has since been replaced by the Rucker 5.0, which adds a contoured lumbar pad and magnetic internal pockets. During the sale, the Basic Rucker's discount made the price gap between the two much wider than usual.

GORUCK's Sand Ruck Plates were in the sale, at a little under 30% off. They are sand-filled fabric bags with a 1000D CORDURA outer and three internal chambers to limit bulging. The 10 lb size measures about 8.5 by 11.5 by 1.5 inches, close to a Standard plate's footprint, and GORUCK says they fit the Rucker 4.0's plate pockets and the Standard Ruck Plate Carrier. A fabric shell avoids the hard edges of a steel plate, but a soft load is less fixed in place than a rigid one.

The GR1 also stayed at full price. It's GORUCK's everyday-carry pack: 1000D CORDURA, a padded laptop compartment and no hip belt included. Our GR1 review covers carrying it with a 20 lb plate. If your rucks double as commutes or travel, the GR1 handles both jobs. If the pack is mainly for plate training, a dedicated Rucker holds the plate higher on your back.

Other items in the sale included the Basic Rucker, Curved Ruck Plates, the Rucking Weight Vest, Ballistic Trainers, Rough Runners, the GR2 and several bags and apparel items. Those prices have ended; check goruck.com for current offers. Amazon listings of GORUCK items may be priced differently from goruck.com.

The non-GORUCK alternatives that still make sense

If price is the main constraint, these alternatives are worth comparing at any time of year.

The 5.11 Rush 12 2.0 is a 24L pack in 1050D water-repellent nylon, with MOLLE webbing on the front, sides and shoulder straps and more exterior pockets than a Rucker. 5.11 lists a contoured yoke strap and a sternum strap but no hip belt, and the pack has no dedicated plate pocket, so a plate has to be secured inside. That makes it a reasonable value choice for lighter training rucks. For heavier plate work, a pack with a dedicated elevated pocket holds the weight in a fixed position.

Rogue's Echo Weight Vest Plates come in pairs of 5, 10 or 20 lb plates, each curved and about 11.3 by 9.3 inches. Rogue lists them for its Echo vest, its plate carrier and similar vests such as the 5.11 TacTec. That footprint is slightly wider than GORUCK's 9 by 11.5 inch Standard plates, so measure a ruck's pocket before assuming they fit. A pair splits the weight across two plates, which suits a two-pocket vest better than a single-pocket pack. Wrap them in foam or a towel if you carry them inside a bag.

The Osprey Tempest Velocity 20 is a women's-specific fast-hiking pack with up to four inches of torso adjustment, a running-vest-style harness, a padded stretch hipbelt and an external hydration sleeve. It is a hiking pack rather than a dedicated rucking pack. If GORUCK's unisex sizing doesn't suit you, its fit and torso adjustment are the reasons to look at it. Use a compact, flexible load such as a tightly packed sandbag rather than assuming a rigid plate will fit its hydration sleeve.

When to buy, when to wait, when to skip

A deep sale tempts a "buy everything" reflex. If a similar sale returns, decide by where you actually are as a rucker.

Buy if:

You've been rucking consistently for three or more months with budget gear and have a fit problem you've confirmed over several sessions (shoulder hot spots, torso length mismatch, plate shifting in a non-pocket bag).

You're committed to an upcoming event and the gear you'd otherwise buy at full price is deeply discounted.

You're replacing a worn-out pack and the upgrade fits your budget at the sale price.

Wait if:

You started rucking in the last 30 days. Your preferences may change as you learn what matters to you.

The sale doesn't include the specific item you'd buy at full price. A discount on something you wouldn't have bought isn't a saving.

Skip if:

You don't ruck often enough for premium gear to pay off. Gear you use four times a month does more for you than premium gear you use once.

The closest item to your need is branded apparel rather than a pack or plate. Apparel is the easiest impulse buy and the least important item in a rucker's kit.

What new ruckers should do

The temptation with a deep sale is to buy the full system at once. We'd push back on that.

Start with one pack and one load. Train consistently for 30 days. If rucking sticks, the gear you want at month two may differ from the gear that looked obvious in week one. A single budget pack you use is better value than premium gear you don't.

The right starter combination depends on body size and intended use. Get one pack that fits, get weight that sits securely in it, and start rucking. The pack and weight decisions are paired: a dedicated Rucker takes plates made for its pocket, while a 5.11 Rush 12 or a hiking pack such as the Tempest Velocity 20 needs a load whose size and shape match the actual compartment.

The accessories a sale rarely covers (insoles, technical socks, a headlamp for early rucks) often decide whether rucking stays comfortable. Socks and shoes that prevent blisters can matter more than the pack, so don't spend the whole gear budget on a sale-day pack.
