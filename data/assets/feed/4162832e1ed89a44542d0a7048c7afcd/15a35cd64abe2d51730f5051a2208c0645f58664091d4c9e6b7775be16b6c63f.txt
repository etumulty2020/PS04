Lems ZENSPEED+ Review: A Foot-Shaped Road Running Shoe That Doesn't Stop at the Pavement

We ran over 100 miles in this 5 mm drop, wide-toe-box shoe on roads, gravel, and dirt across Colorado and Utah.

October 7, 2026Home → Gear Reviews → Running

Our verdict

The Lems ZENSPEED+ is a comfortable and lightweight road running shoe that applies Lems' natural foot-shape principles to running.

Like other Lems shoes, they put your foot in a more natural position while you run. They come in two widths, have a wide toe box, and a 5 mm heel-to-toe drop. The midsole feels responsive, yet grounded, with a full-coverage rubber outsole that grips better than most road running shoes.

I tested the ZENSPEED+ while running on pavement, gravel, and dirt roads in Colorado and Utah during the summer and early fall. I’ve put over 100 miles on them so far, in conditions ranging from hot, dry summer afternoons in Denver to cooler 50-degree fall mornings in Moab and Escalante, Utah. I’ve worn them on purely pavement runs and longer runs that start on pavement but lead to dirt roads and smooth singletrack trails. These aren’t a trail shoe or even a “gravel” shoe, but they work well on a variety of surfaces, as long as you aren’t trying to get too rowdy. They’re also a great option for everyday wear, walking, travel, light hiking, and gym use.

The ZENSPEED+ are extremely comfortable everyday running shoes. They have a cork insole that’s very comfortable and grips the bottom of your feet better than foam insoles, even if your feet are sweaty. After using this cork footbed, I honestly wish every insole was like this.

The ZENSPEED+ aren't high-tech, and they don't try to be, but that’s why I like them so much. They aren't the fastest, and they don't have the most cushion. But they’ll be plenty fast for everyday training runs, long runs, and speed workouts, and the responsive EVA and PEBA midsole gives plenty of cushion and energy return as you stride.

Lems ZENSPEED+

The biggest downside is that these shoes aren’t for everyone. If you prefer max-cushion, bouncy running shoes, look elsewhere. If you’re looking for a shoe with a rocker that makes you feel like you’re floating on clouds down the road, these are not the shoes for that. I don’t see this as a downside, because a shoe that encourages a more natural stride matters for building and maintaining overall foot strength, among other benefits. However, that might not be how you feel about running shoes. Fortunately, we have guides to women’s running shoes and road running shoes for men if you’re looking for a different style.

If you're looking for an all-around running shoe that bucks many modern, maximalist running-shoe trends, I think the Lems ZENSPEED+ is a great option.

I tested the Lems ZENSPEED+ while running on paved roads, dirt roads, gravel roads, and smooth singletrack trails in Colorado and Utah during the summer and early fall. I also wore them while walking around, working out, and during daily life.

Performance in the field

Fit and Comfort

The ZENSPEED+ running shoes are comfortable right out of the box. When I received my test pair, I put them on to check the fit, then immediately went on an 8-mile run with no issues.

I tested them in size 10 wide, and they don’t feel too loose or sloppy through the midfoot. I typically wear a men’s size 9.5, but I’ve worn other Lems shoes in size 10.

If you're trying Lems shoes for the first time, pay attention to the width options. The ZENSPEED+ comes in D width, similar to the original Lems wide fit, and a narrower B width, which Lems calls medium. I find the wide size feels similar to the Altra Torin or Altra Timp—wider through the toe box but narrower through the midfoot and heel than the Altra Lone Peak. I expect the B width version fits similarly to the women’s version of these shoes, too.

The heel cup locks the back of my foot in place. And the roomy toe box prevents my toes from rubbing against each other during long, hot runs. The longest run I’ve completed with these shoes is 15 miles, and I’ve had no issues with discomfort or chafing.

They have a cork insole that is more comfortable than the insoles in most running shoes. It feels firmer than the soft foam insole commonly found in running shoes, yet still provides enough cushioning without feeling mushy. This insole grips the bottom of your foot better than a typical insole with a knit surface. The cork doesn’t get slippery as your feet sweat, and it doesn’t seem to stink as much as some insoles do either.

The ZENSPEED+ has a wide toe box. It isn’t so wide that your foot slides around, but it is wide enough for your toes to splay naturally. They don’t have quite as wide a toe box as the Altra Lone Peak or Olympus, but they feel about as wide as the Altra Torin 8, for reference.

The midfoot fits my wider-than-average foot well, without so much room that my foot slides around, but also not too tight.

The laces attach to the engineered mesh upper. The tongue attaches to the midsole with elastic strips that wrap around your midfoot. I find I don’t have to tighten these shoes as much as I’d expect, and they still hold my foot in place.

The ZENSPEED+ have a padded ankle collar and heel cup that keeps your heel locked in place. They have a second set of eyelets for the heel-lock lacing technique, but I find these shoes hold my heel in place without any special lacing patterns.

Midsole and Cushioning

The ZENSPEED+ has 31 mm of stack in the heel and 26 mm in the forefoot, with a 5 mm drop. You might think of Lems as a zero-drop shoe brand, but they intentionally designed these shoes with a 5 mm drop so they’re more approachable for average runners.

The midsole uses 90% EVA (Ethylene-Vinyl Acetate) and 10% PEBA (Polyether Block Amide). EVA is the foam most commonly used in running shoe midsoles, and PEBA, common in “super shoes,” adds extra bounce.

The cork insole provides a subtle amount of extra cushioning as well. Much like a Birkenstock sandal, which has a cork midsole, the cork insole in the ZENSPEED+ will conform to your foot over time, making it more comfortable as they break in.

This foam combination feels responsive yet grounded. It's not a soft, bouncy midsole, and it doesn't have the most cushion. But it’s very comfortable and has a lot more bounce than a minimalist shoe.

Compared to other trail and road running shoes, the ZENSPEED+ has average cushioning. They have about as much cushion as low-stack models, such as the Altra Experience Flow 3 (32-28 mm) and Altra Torin 8 (30 mm zero drop).

They're much less cushioned than shoes with 37+ mm of stack height, such as the HOKA Clifton 10 (42-34 mm men’s) or the On Cloudsurfer (37-28 mm). They also have less heel-to-toe drop than these models.

The ZENSPEED+ midsole absorbs impact while providing a quick, snappy return. They have less heel-to-toe drop than many road running shoes, but the extra 5 mm of heel cushioning is enough to cushion your footfall if you strike with your heel first.

These shoes don’t feel mushy or like you’re running on a cloud. The midsole feels firm and light on impact, yet provides a subtle, predictable bounce that keeps your feet moving forward as you push off the ground.

On longer runs over pavement, they provide enough cushion for most people. However, newbie runners or those accustomed to higher-stack shoes, such as the HOKA Clifton or On Cloudsurfer, should gradually increase the distance they run in the ZENSPEED+ so their feet can adjust to the lower stack and cushion. The ZENSPEED+ is a great option to add to a running shoe rotation, but the medium cushion and lower stack height might take some getting used to.

Stability

The ZENSPEED+ are very stable. I’ve run in these shoes on pavement, gravel, and dirt surfaces. They feel firm and planted on the ground beneath you, even when that ground is uneven. They aren’t as grippy as a trail shoe designed for off-road use, but they feel much more stable than many road running shoes do on off-camber ground.

As an aside, in the running shoe world, a “stability shoe” is designed to support the foot and reduce excessive inward rolling, known as overpronation. The ZENSPEED+ isn't a stability shoe in that sense—it’s a neutral shoe.

The ZENSPEED+ is a neutral support shoe. It isn’t designed to prevent overpronation, but it provides more support than a minimalist shoe.

It has a minimal cork insole that provides subtle arch support, and the contoured midsole supports your arch. Overall, this shoe doesn’t provide as much arch support as some running shoes because it allows natural foot flexion.

The ZENSPEED+ has excellent lateral support. The mesh upper and comfortable laces keep your foot firmly in position as you push off to either side.

Some running shoes feel like they're about to tip over during sharp lateral movements, but the ZENSPEED+ sits lower to the ground, with an upper that keeps your foot in place. This makes them ideal for workouts, speed work with sharp cuts, and other activities that don’t involve simply running forward.

Ride

The ZENSPEED+ has a grounded, smooth, and responsive ride. I like how these shoes feel compared to many road-oriented running and gravel shoes. They have just enough cushion to pad your footfall without feeling like they have too much, so you can still feel the ground, unlike many max-cushion shoes. Groundfeel is helpful in maintaining traction because it helps your feet know when they need to grip firmer on uneven pavement or loose gravel. They have a smooth rocker profile, which isn’t nearly as extreme as the MetaRocker found on HOKA running shoes, for instance.

They’re not as bouncy as some shoes, such as the HOKA Clifton. But they feel slightly softer than the Altra EgoMax midsole found on the Torin 8. But the ZENSPEED+ doesn’t feel too soft, either. They compress subtly with every footfall, and rebound predictably as the subtle rocker rolls your foot forward and you push off.

Outsole, Grip, and Traction

The ZENSPEED+ has a full-coverage outsole made from a 3.5 mm strip of injected rubber. It provides better grip than most road running shoes. It's not quite grippy enough for rough trails, but I like how it grips on dirt roads.

The outsole has an arrow-shaped tread pointing toward the front of the shoe. This tread grips surprisingly well on dirt, gravel, and wet pavement. I’ve taken these shoes on a fair amount of smooth, hard-packed dirt roads and trails, and they aren’t as grippy as a trail shoe with deep lugs, but they’re about as grippy as a gravel running shoe.

These shoes shine on road runs that mix pavement and gravel. They're less at home on super-rough dirt roads, where the outsole and midsole aren't as protective or as grippy as in a true trail shoe. However, they’re well suited for longer runs where you don’t know exactly what the road surface will be like.

I’ve used the ZENSPEED+ on pavement, sidewalks, smooth gravel, rocky dirt roads, and hard-packed dirt surfaces with some sandy patches. They work well for all these types of roads.

The ZENSPEED+ performs well in wet and dry conditions. The full-coverage rubber outsole grips well on dry pavement and hard-packed dirt, and the shoes feel solid when changing direction quickly. I’ve mostly used them in dry, summertime conditions in Colorado and Utah, but I’ve never slipped on patches of wet pavement, either.

Versatility

The ZENSPEED+ is a road shoe that can do so much more than just roads. They're definitely more road-oriented than an off-road shoe, but they work well for running out your front door and onto far-out dirt roads. They’re also a great option for everyday wear, walking, travel, light hiking, and gym use.

Responsiveness

The midsole feels responsive without feeling mushy. The 10% PEBA adds extra bounce without making it feel unnatural. The ZENSPEED+ isn't built to be the fastest shoe on the road, but it’s lightweight, and the midsole feels firm with a subtle energy return as you push off.

Warmth

The ZENSPEED+ has a breathable mesh upper that won’t keep your feet warm in cold weather. If you plan to use these in cold weather, consider thicker socks or check out some other tips for winter running.

Break-in Time

I pulled these shoes out of the box, put them on to check the fit, and then immediately went on an 8-mile run over pavement and dirt. They’re very comfortable right out of the box, and I had no issues on that first run or on any subsequent runs or walks. However, if you aren’t accustomed to running shoes with a lower stack height and less cushion, you may want to ease into them gradually by starting with shorter runs and alternating with your old pair of running shoes.

Upper and Breathability

The ZENSPEED+ shoes are very breathable. I’ve worn them in temperatures up to 100 degrees, and they’ve never caused my feet to overheat. I’ve also had no issues with hotspots or blisters, even when running in very hot conditions.

Durability and Longevity

So far, I've run over 100 miles on roads of all sorts in these shoes. The upper shows no signs of tearing or abrasion, the midsole still feels like new, and the outsole shows no premature wear.

After 100+ miles, the midsole still feels as good as new. I would expect this midsole to last at least as long as other running shoes with an EVA midsole. For reference, most running shoe midsoles last about 300 to 500 miles, depending on the terrain and other factors.

Also, the ZENSPEED+ has no exposed midsole foam on the tread, which prevents rocks or other sharp objects underfoot from tearing the midsole and shortening the shoes' longevity.

The engineered mesh upper feels durable enough for road running conditions. If you start using these on rocky trails, they might not last as long, though. For what it’s worth, I’ve worn my pair on a fair amount of rocky singletrack trails, and they’re still going strong.

They have TPU reinforcement around the toe box, and these overlays wrap along the base of the upper and around the heel as well. This helps protect the mesh upper in higher wear areas. So far, the uppers on my test pair of the ZENSPEED+ have no tears or abrasions.

The full-coverage rubber outsole is firm, so it won’t wear out as quickly as some other rubber types. Also, because there is more rubber on the outsole, it distributes wear over a larger area, increasing longevity.

Price and Value

At $175 per pair, the ZENSPEED+ is priced about at par with other running shoes. But if you’re looking for a mid-drop road-running shoe that’s versatile across different terrain types, I think this is a good value.
