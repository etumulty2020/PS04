Saucony Ride 19 Review: The Reliable Daily Trainer Gets Quietly Better

A balanced, no-frills neutral road shoe for every pace, now a touch softer and snappier and still $145

October 7th, 2026Home > Gear Reviews > Running

Our verdict

The Saucony Ride 19 is a neutral road running shoe that balances moderate cushion and responsiveness, making it a reliable, trusty daily trainer for beginner to elite runners. The Saucony Ride line has been a favorite among Treeline testers for years, and the latest model, which only made minor adjustments to the midsole and upper compared to the Saucony Ride 18, remains true to its roots.

Saucony updated the PWRRUN+ foam midsole so the Ride 19 has more cushion and more responsive turnover than previous models, but only minimally so, which we appreciate. We could only tell the difference when directly comparing the Ride 19 and Ride 18 side by side. And we’re grateful, because this shoe has long been one of our favorite daily trainers, and we appreciate that it remains consistent and available at a reasonable price.

Saucony Ride 19

We tested the Saucony Ride 19 while running on paved paths, gravel and dirt roads, and even non-technical trails throughout Colorado during the late spring and summer months. Runs ranged from easy, recovery paces to uptempo runs and strides to assess turnover and energy return. With over 100 miles in these shoes, we also tested them alongside other road running shoes to directly compare fit, cushion, responsiveness, traction, breathability, support, overall comfort, and more.

This shoe is not the most plush, nor is it the snappiest. If you want a maximum-cushioned trainer, midsole tech that encourages propulsion, or a low-drop or zero-drop shoe, this is not the road shoe for you. But if you want a no-frills, reliable, capable road running shoe that’s versatile across a range of paces at a good price, the Saucony Ride 19 is hard to beat.

How we tested

We tested the men’s and women’s Saucony Ride 19 in regular sizes. Our men’s and women’s testers have covered over 100 miles in these shoes, ranging from 3-mile recovery days to 10-mile long runs. To test turnover and responsiveness, we did light workouts, pick-ups, and post-run strides.

Testing occurred mostly during the hot summer months in Colorado, with our testers primarily using the Ride 19 on asphalt roads, cement city sidewalks, paved pedestrian paths, gravel paths, and dirt roads. We also ventured onto some non-technical trails.

To directly compare the updates to the new model, we ran with the Saucony Ride 19 on one foot and the Saucony Ride 18 on the other. We also tested the Ride 19 alongside similar road running shoes we’re currently testing, like the Saucony Triumph 24, adidas Adizero EVO SL, Salomon Aero Blaze 4, HOKA Mach 7, HOKA Clifton 11, and others from our Best Men’s Road Running Shoes and Best Women’s Road Running Shoes guides. This allowed us to directly compare cushion, responsiveness, ride, traction, grip, support, fit, general comfort, and more.

Performance in the field

Fit and feel

The Saucony Ride 19 fits true to size. The shoes are available in regular and wide sizes, in 5-12 (women’s) and 7-15 (men’s). Like most road running shoes, it doesn’t have a particularly wide toe box, but both our narrow-to-regular-footed tester and our average-to-wide-footed tester had no issues with the standard fit. The heel feels snug and secure, and the interior is plush, which aids in overall comfort. In our experience, Sauconys tend to consistently fit most foot types comfortably.

Because of their comfortable fit, these shoes required no break-in period, and we experienced no hot spots. Other than the 8 mm drop, which might be high for some folks, this is one of the easiest road shoes to comfortably run in out of the box.

Ride and responsiveness

Since the Saucony Ride 19 is a no-frills road running shoe, its midsole doesn’t feel as springy as those with more midsole tech. The PWRRUN+ foam midsole is plush and responsive enough to give you a smooth ride that feels balanced and unremarkable—but that’s what we really like about these shoes.

They’re cushioned, but you don’t sink into them as you do with some plush, recovery-oriented road running shoes. They’re responsive, but not in a way that propels you forward or makes you feel like you’re working out.

The ride is balanced and smooth. This makes them best suited as daily training shoes that still work well for easier recovery runs, long runs, and light workouts like tempos, Fartleks, or marathon-pace runs.

Cushioning

As stated above, the PWRRUN+ foam offers cushion, but not an alarming or noticeable amount (unless you’re coming up from a minimalist shoe). Saucony updated their proprietary PWRRUN+ foam to have a slightly softer cushion and a touch more responsiveness than the previous version in the 18s. The difference is negligible unless you’re testing the shoes side by side, and even then, it’s small. But we’re grateful—the Saucony Ride has been a consistently reliable, high-quality road running shoe for years, and we hope it stays that way.

The moderate level of cushion in this shoe is plenty for long runs. We ran up to 10 miles in them on a single run and never felt fatigued or uncomfortable. The midsole should last roughly 300-500 miles, depending on factors such as how much impact it takes (e.g., your weight or how much downhill running you do), your foot strike pattern, and the terrain you run on.

Traction and grip

The rubber outsole feels like that of any other road running shoe, providing plenty of traction and grip on road runs, and it performs well on gravel and non-technical terrain. But it’s not designed for trail running, and we wouldn’t necessarily recommend it for that.

In testing, we mostly ran on roads, but did venture onto dirt roads and gravel paths. The Saucony Ride 19s performed just fine crossing over. But if you’ll be running mostly on gravel roads, you may want to check out our Best Gravel Running Shoes guide.

Breathability and upper

The upper is made of mesh textile and synthetic leather. The mesh allows for airflow and makes it plenty breathable, and the synthetic leather adds some durability so the shoe lasts longer. Most of our testing was done during a very hot summer in Colorado, with temperatures up to 100 degrees, and our feet felt just as well ventilated in these as in just about any other road running shoe we’ve tested.

Saucony updated the Ride 19s with a new engineered mesh upper designed to be more breathable and stretchier for a more inclusive fit. This update isn’t super noticeable—unless you’re comparing them directly with the 18s side by side, in which case the stretch may be more apparent. We appreciate the gradual direction Saucony is taking this shoe.

Stability and support

The Saucony Ride 19s are a neutral shoe, which means they do not offer stability or motion control for overpronation. But in terms of feeling stable and supported underfoot while running, these shoes offer plenty of lateral stability. Our women’s tester rolls her ankles a lot, but has never rolled her ankles in these shoes.

If you want to add more stability, you can replace the insoles with custom orthotics or aftermarket insoles.

Weight and efficiency

The women’s Ride 19 weighs 7.7 oz per shoe, and the men’s weighs 8.9 oz per shoe. This is pretty lightweight for a road running shoe, though not the lightest if that’s your priority. In any case, the shoes never felt heavy, and our feet never felt fatigued, even over long runs.

Durability and longevity

The Saucony Ride 19s are expected to last 300-500 miles, like most road running shoes. This depends on factors like how much weight/impact you put on the midsole, the terrain you run on, your gait and whether your toe wears on the toe box, and how hard you are on these shoes in general. While 300-500 miles is the general guideline for how long a pair of running shoes will last, we think the Saucony Ride 19 is likely a little hardier than lightweight, speed-focused running shoes with more exposed midsoles. And at $145, that makes them a better value than most road running shoes, too.

Features

Drop measurement

The Saucony Ride 19 is a high-drop shoe with an 8 mm drop (or offset).

Stack height

The stack height is 36/28 mm, which is just 1 mm higher than the stack of the Ride 18s. This is pretty average for road running shoes. In testing, it did not feel particularly noticeable, and the shoe feels stable underfoot.

PWRRUN+

The Ride 19 has a PWRRUN+ foam midsole, which balances cushion with responsiveness for a smooth ride. The formula of this foam has been updated since the Ride 18 to be a smidge more cushioned and a smidge more responsive than before, though admittedly our testers could hardly tell the difference.

Lacing system

These shoes use a traditional lacing system, with round, plush laces.

Heel tab design

These shoes don’t have a heel tab to help you pull on the shoes, but they’re easy enough to get on and off that we didn’t miss this feature.

Reflective elements

There are reflective elements at the heel and forefoot. This is helpful for visibility, but if you’ll be running at night, we recommend adding more reflective, hi-vis, or light-up apparel or accessories to be more visible. Check out our Best Reflective Gear for Running in the Dark guide.

Colors

The women’s shoes come in 17 colors and the men’s come in 15 colors. Unfortunately, the wide sizes only come in five, more basic color options.

Sustainability

Saucony has expanded its Run For Good line (unfortunately, not with the Ride), which uses more recycled, renewable, and organic materials. In general, the brand is working toward using more of those materials and eliminating packaging waste. Learn more about their sustainability initiatives on their website.

Who should buy the Saucony Ride 19?

Buy if

Buy the Saucony Ride 19 if you run on paved surfaces and want a neutral daily trainer that balances moderate cushion with responsiveness at a budget-friendly price.

Skip if

Skip the Saucony Ride 19 if you want a road running shoe with maximum cushion, more midsole tech for snappier turnover, or a lower drop. Also skip if you’re looking for a trail running shoe or want a stability shoe for overpronation.

FAQ

Does the Saucony Ride 19 fit true to size?

Yes, in our testing. It comes in regular and wide, and both our narrow-to-regular and average-to-wide testers were comfortable in the regular fit.

What changed from the Ride 18?

With the Ride 19 you get a reformulated PWRRUN+ midsole with slightly more cushion and responsiveness, a stretchier engineered-mesh upper, and 1 mm more stack. We only noticed the differences side by side.

Can you run trails in the Ride 19?

The Ride 19 handled gravel, dirt roads and non-technical trails fine, but it isn't a trail shoe.
