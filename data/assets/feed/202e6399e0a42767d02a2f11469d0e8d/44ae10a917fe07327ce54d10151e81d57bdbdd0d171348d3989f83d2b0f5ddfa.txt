Guy Fieri's fitness routine is a useful example of how loaded walking can fit into a broader exercise habit. It deserves a more careful reading than a celebrity weight-loss headline usually gets.

What Fieri actually described

In a May 2024 interview with Men's Health, Fieri reported losing more than 30 pounds since 2020. His routine included trainer-led HIIT, changes to his eating habits and uphill walks wearing a weighted vest. The writer joined him for one of those walks.

That's a multi-part routine. The interview can't tell us how much of his weight change came from the vest, the hills, the gym sessions or his diet. Our earlier headline about celebrities trading gyms for rucksacks went beyond what this account supported.

A vest walk and a ruck have different equipment

We use rucking to mean walking with a loaded backpack. A weighted vest distributes its load around the torso instead. Both add weight to a walk; their fit, storage and loading options differ.

That distinction matters when you're shopping. A vest may suit someone who wants to add load without carrying supplies. A pack gives you room for water, clothing and the other things you actually need outdoors. Our vest-versus-rucksack comparison explains those tradeoffs.

The interview isn't a product test or an endorsement of the gear below. These are options from our own buying guides.

What you can reasonably take from the story

The useful question is where walking fits in your week. A familiar route, a manageable session and a reason to go outside give you something you can repeat. You can work on that before buying more equipment.

Adding weight can raise the effort of a walk, but a heavier vest isn't a shortcut to someone else's result. Pace, hills, body size, food intake and how regularly you exercise all matter. A celebrity transformation supplies a story, not a controlled experiment.

For a broader look at the evidence, see our rucking benefits guide and weight-loss results explainer. We separate measured findings from estimates there, including why calorie arithmetic can't promise a particular number on the scale.

Choosing equipment for your own walks

Start with the use case. If you want a vest, compare adjustment range and fit rather than chasing the biggest advertised capacity. If you want to carry supplies, a comfortable pack is the more practical starting point.

The CAP adjustable vest is one option in our vest coverage. The Rucker 5.0 is a dedicated pack with elevated plate storage. Neither purchase is a requirement for building a walking habit.
