Choose for what you need to carry

A rucksack gives you room for water, a jacket and other gear as well as weight. A vest keeps the load around your torso and may be easier to manage during an exercise circuit. That's a useful distinction without pretending either is universally better for your body.

Does the same weight burn the same calories?

Both add mass to the walk. That alone doesn't establish identical energy use. Placement, fit, restriction, pace and the person can matter.

Our calorie calculator uses the Pandolf load-carriage model. It has no vest-versus-pack input. Matching numbers from that calculator are therefore a model limitation, not a head-to-head experiment proving the devices equivalent.

Wang and colleagues' 2026 review pooled nine trials with 368 middle-aged and older adults. Vest-plus-exercise programs improved some bone outcomes versus usual activity, with low-to-moderate certainty. Against exercise without a vest, the extra bone benefit wasn't clear in the one trial contributing that comparison. These weren't trials of packs against vests, or a single walking-only protocol. Our bone-density guide explains that distinction.

Barman and colleagues measured spinal movement in 47 adults carrying a light front load versus no load. It changed trunk position and lumbar curvature. They didn't compare a balanced walking vest with a backpack, so the study can't prove that a vest fixes posture.

Orr and colleagues compared three military packs in 12 soldiers carrying 30 kg. Selected physiological and performance measures didn't differ significantly. A small study of three backpacks isn't evidence that all packs and vests feel or perform the same.

Fit and loading matter more than category slogans

A pack sits behind you, and your body adjusts to the load. A vest may carry weight front and back, around the torso or in a fixed pattern. Designs differ; neither category guarantees an upright spine or a particular distribution of muscle work.

A suitable load-bearing hip belt can transfer some pack weight away from the shoulders. Not every waist strap does that, and there isn't a threshold at which all vests become uncomfortable while all packs stay comfortable. Heavier vests rest largely on the shoulders, so fit matters more as the load climbs.

Both can become uncomfortable. Remove or reduce load when it changes gait or causes symptoms. Existing shoulder, back, heart, lung or balance problems call for individual guidance rather than a different product category as an assumed fix.

When to choose a pack

For a longer walk with supplies, a pack is the practical choice. It also matches the equipment used in many ruck events. Check the actual event requirements rather than treating every obstacle race as a backpack event.

The 5.11 RUSH 24 offers cargo space, but it doesn't have a dedicated ruck-plate pocket or an included load-bearing hip belt. Secure the weight properly. A purpose-built rucking backpack may offer a more convenient loading system.

When to choose a vest

A vest can be convenient for a short walk or exercises where you want less cargo bulk. Check that it stays put during the movements you intend to do. Some have removable small weights, some take specific plates, and others have weight sewn in. Don't assume you can reduce a fixed 20 or 30 lb vest after buying it.

Our CAP Barbell review covers an adjustable option; the Hyperwear Hyper Vest PRO offers a different low-profile design. For fit alternatives, see the women's gear guide. A women's pattern may help fit, but the label doesn't guarantee comfort.

Do you need both?

Only if they solve different problems for you. A pack for gear and a vest for circuits can make sense. You can also keep using one comfortable setup. Equipment variety is optional; managing the total workload still matters if you alternate.
