What a GORUCK Tough actually is

A GORUCK Tough is a 10-12 hour, cadre-led team event built around loaded movement, physical work, and group tasks. GORUCK describes the Tough as a challenge rather than a race, and the route, distance, start time, terrain, and class-specific items can vary.

The packing list attached to your registration is non-negotiable. This guide was checked against an official GORUCK Tough registration page on August 31, 2026, but it is not a substitute for the page and emails tied to your class.

This guide separates the baseline items visible on that official page from additions that can make a long event more manageable. It is research-based: product judgments come from current manufacturer specifications, official event guidance and editorial analysis.

If you are buying today, buy in this order

Most people over-shop the small stuff and under-decide the big stuff. For a Tough, your money should go first toward the items that protect your start line, your feet, and your ability to keep moving after hour six.

Three event kits that actually make sense

Budget start, no wasted money. Use the pack you own if it carries the required weight safely, then add a compatible secured weight, event-approved reflectivity, Darn Tough socks, Body Glide, and the hydration capacity specified for your event. This gets you training without pretending a full premium setup is required on day one.

Buy-once, use-everywhere setup. GORUCK GR1, GORUCK Ruck Plate, Garmin Instinct 3 Solar, Petzl Actik Core, and Salomon XA Pro. This costs more, but every item still makes sense after the event for training, travel, and daily use.

Buy, recommend or skip: the whole list at a glance

Everything below is sorted into three buckets. "Official-page baseline" reflects the GORUCK registration page cited above. "Confirm for your class" covers items that vary. "Editorial add-on" means it can solve a practical problem but is not presented as a universal requirement.

Baseline items on the official page we checked

1. A rucksack

The cited event page requires a rucksack with reflectivity, not a particular brand. The pack must hold the assigned load securely, carry 3L of hydration, and stay controllable during group work. A purpose-built event pack can make that easier, but it is not permission to skip fit and training.

Default: GORUCK GR1 26L. A common Tough pack with broad everyday use.

Purpose-built alternative: GORUCK Rucker 5.0 in 20L or 25L ($150-300 band). Its two internal plate pockets and large lumbar pad are built for long training and event loads. Full Rucker vs GR1 comparison →

Budget starter: 5.11 RUSH 24 2.0. Works, but its load geometry is less event-specific than the Rucker's elevated plate system.

2. A ruck plate (the weight)

On the official event page checked for this update, required weight depended on bodyweight:

Under 150 lb: 20 lb plate

Over 150 lb: 30 lb plate

That is one event page, not a permanent system-wide promise. Always use the load shown on your own registration page.

Default: GORUCK Ruck Plate (20 or 30 lb Standard, $50-150 band). Standard plates fit the Rucker's Short pocket, and GORUCK recommends the 30 lb Standard plate for the GR1.

Budget: Yes4All cast-iron ruck plates. A pair of 10 lb cast-iron plates that makes sense while you are still testing whether events are for you; check the dimensions against your pack and the event's plate rules before counting on it for inspection.

Non-GORUCK pack: Hyperwear FlexLoad. Flexible weights that adapt to packs without a dedicated plate pocket.

3. Rucksack reflectivity

The official page we checked requires the rucksack to have reflectivity. A reflective belt may satisfy that requirement for some classes, while another class may specify a panel or a different setup. Match the wording on your event page instead of buying from a generalized checklist.

Cheapest viable: Glow Belt Military Reflective PT Belt. Orange, yellow, or blue are commonly available.

Upgrade: Noxgear Tracer2 illuminated vest. More than a Tough requires, but useful for anyone who rucks in traffic regularly.

4. Photo ID

State ID or passport. The official page we checked lists photo ID as required.

5. 3L hydration and required medication

The cited page lists a 3L hydration minimum and any medication you might need. A bladder is the usual way to carry it; test it under load before the event and protect medication according to its storage instructions.

6. Class-specific items

Cash, a headlamp, a flag, a team weight, or other shared equipment may appear in class communications. They were not universal required items on the official page used for this update, so treat them as "confirm" items rather than permanent rules.

Strongly recommended items

These aren't on the official baseline list, but they solve problems that come up on most long events.

Footwear

A Tough can cover sand, water, pavement and uneven ground over 10 to 12 hours, so footwear deserves more thought than most packing-list items get. The Salomon XA Pro 3D V9 GTX is a common choice: an aggressive Contagrip outsole for mud and loose ground, GORE-TEX waterproofing and a stable platform for carrying awkward loads. Break in whatever you choose with several loaded training sessions before the event.

Foot care kit

Blisters are one of the most preventable ways to have a hard night. Pre-tape your hot spots before the event starts and keep refill supplies in a small zip-lock inside your pack.

Leukotape P. Goes over hot spots before blisters form. Pre-tape your heels, your arches, and the balls of your feet.

Trail Toes or Body Glide. Anti-friction balm on the feet, thighs, nipples, and anywhere else straps meet skin.

ENGO blister patches. Stick to the inside of your shoe to reduce friction at known hot-spot locations.

Moleskin. Emergency blister cover if a blister forms mid-event.

Hydration system

Tough events last 10-12 hours, and the official page checked for this update lists 3L of hydration. Do not assume a resupply schedule; train with the system you will carry.

Source WXP 3L bladder. A military-style bladder with a push-pull Storm valve that has a built-in shut-off. GORUCK recommends its 3L Source bladder for the Rucker.

Backup bottle: Nalgene 32 oz or HydraPak Stow 1L collapsible. Stuff in a side pocket for refills.

LMNT Electrolytes. Carry packets for refills if they agree with your stomach; do not try a new drink mix for the first time during the event.

Rite in the Rain notebook + Sharpie

Cadre sometimes set tasks that involve remembering or writing things down, such as numbers or teammates' names. A regular notebook turns to pulp in the first water obstacle.

Rite in the Rain #946. A compact waterproof notebook that fits a pack lid pocket.

Waterproof Sharpie. Keep one clipped to the notebook.

Windbreaker / rain shell

Packable, lightweight, just enough to block wind. Many Toughs run through the night, temperatures drop, and you will spend time standing still during team tasks.

Dry bag

A simple roll-top dry bag inside your pack keeps your notebook, ID, cash, phone and any electronics usable after a water obstacle.

Sea to Summit Ultra-Sil Dry Sack 8L. A compact way to keep the dry layer separate.

Headlamp

A 10 to 12 hour event usually includes hours of darkness. Hands-free light makes any two-handed task easier (rope work, reading the notebook, pulling gear from the pack in the dark).

Petzl Actik Core. Rechargeable with enough output for night movement and close work.

Budget: Black Diamond Spot 400. A simpler light with replaceable-power flexibility.

Calorie-dense food

Food needs vary sharply with body size, effort, weather, and what you can tolerate while moving. Use long training rucks to determine a simple fueling rhythm, then confirm whether your event page places any restrictions on what you carry.

Honey Stinger Organic Energy Chews. Easy to chew while walking.

GU Energy Gels. Compact carbohydrates for the middle miles; test them in training first.

Bars: Cliff Bar, RXBar, Honey Stinger Waffle. Pick what you have actually eaten during training rucks.

Small jar of peanut butter or nut butter packets. Slow-burn calorie density that holds up at hour 8.

First aid kit

A small dedicated kit for personal use. Team first aid is good, but you do not want to be the person digging through someone else's kit at mile 15.

Adventure Medical Kits Ultralight .7. Compact enough to carry without turning the ruck into a medic bag.

Israeli Emergency Bandage 6". For the serious-wound scenario. Not typical at Toughs but compact enough to carry.

Cold-weather layers (Oct-April events)

Merino wool base layer (Smartwool Merino 150)

Fleece mid-layer or beanie

Work gloves (the official page we checked recommends working gloves; add an insulated pair for cold nights)

Wool socks (Darn Tough Light Hiker)

Hot-weather gear (May-Sept events)

Buff or neck gaiter (sun, sweat, breathing through it in dust)

Moisture-wicking t-shirt

Electrolyte supplements (LMNT 30-pack)

Additional water bottle for refills

Pants

Temperatures can swing over a 10 to 12 hour event, and you may not get a chance to change. Convertible pants solve that without adding a layer to carry: zip the legs off when the work heats up, zip them back on when you stop moving and the sweat turns cold. The Columbia Silver Ridge Convertible is a common pick here: ripstop nylon, a gusseted crotch, and cheap enough that shredding a knee does not hurt. GORUCK's own Simple Pants are the tougher option if you would rather have one pair that survives everything than legs that come off.

Small additions worth deciding in training

These solve common problems, but they are editorial suggestions rather than a claim about what every finisher carries:

Extra pair of socks (a mid-event change helps wet or sweaty feet)

Ziploc bags (for wet gear segregation, trash, organization)

Sunglasses (Goodr or any cheap polarized)

Duct tape strip wrapped around a pen (gear repair field-expedient)

Small multi-tool or knife

Items you can usually leave at home

Common over-packs to question unless your event specifically calls for them:

Anything heavier than needed (you carry everything all day)

Large first aid kits you are not trained to use

Camping gear (it is not a thru-hike)

Anything fragile (water features + team challenges = broken gear)

Keep a dry change of clothes with your transportation for after the event; the official page checked for this update recommends it.

Event-day timeline

2 weeks out:

Recheck your registration page and event emails, then close any actual gaps

Use your event footwear and pack on several progressively longer training sessions

Complete a long rehearsal with the event load only if it fits your current program and recovery

Practice pre-taping your feet

72 hours out:

Hydrate normally and consistently rather than forcing excess water

Eat familiar meals and avoid last-minute fueling experiments

Protect sleep as much as your schedule allows

Event day:

Eat a real breakfast 2-3 hours before start

Pre-tape feet, apply Body Glide to chafe points

Arrive 30+ minutes early for check-in

Carry the official packing list printed out in a dry bag

Post-event:

Rehydrate and eat according to thirst, appetite, weather, and any medical guidance you follow

Change into dry clothes and inspect your feet

Resume loaded training when soreness, gait, and normal daily movement have recovered rather than obeying an arbitrary countdown
