On May 8, 2026, GORUCK released the Rucker 5.0, the fifth version of the dedicated rucking pack it first launched in 2016. It replaces the Rucker 4.0, which was on sale by early 2022. The 5.0 comes in 20L and 25L, and GORUCK lists two changes from the 4.0: a contoured 30mm lumbar pad and magnetic closures on the internal pockets in place of hook-and-loop.

Launch coverage from HiConsumption and Gear Patrol described the same two changes, and Gear Patrol also noted redesigned shoulder straps. The useful question for a buyer is whether those changes matter for the way you ruck.

This analysis is based on GORUCK's published specifications and launch coverage from HiConsumption and Gear Patrol. For carrying impressions, see our hands-on Rucker 5.0 review and Rucker 5.0 vs 4.0 upgrade guide.

What's new in the Rucker 5.0

GORUCK's own "new for 5.0" list has two items. Several other details carry over from the 4.0.

Magnetic closures replace hook-and-loop on the internal pockets. Recessed magnets close the two pockets inside the main compartment. They work one-handed, make less noise than hook-and-loop and don't collect lint, sand or pet hair the way the hook side does.

A redesigned lumbar pad. GORUCK lists 30mm high-density EVA foam, contoured to follow the curve of the lower back. Launch coverage described it as dual-density, with a slightly recessed center. The 4.0's pad was flatter and thinner.

Materials and frame. The main panels are 1000D CORDURA, as on the 4.0, with 210D HT CORDURA on the back panel. The 3mm frame sheet carried over from the 4.0.

Plate storage and sizes. The 5.0 has two internal plate pockets, one sized for Standard plates and one for Long plates, and GORUCK rates it for up to 75 lb of plates. It comes in 20L and 25L. The 4.0 was also sold in a 15L.

What carried over from the 4.0: the 1000D CORDURA shell, the frame sheet, dual elevated plate pockets, an optional hip belt sold separately, and the SCARS Lifetime Guarantee. Sources: GORUCK's Rucker 5.0 product page and retailer listings for the 4.0.

The magnetic closures

The magnets replace hook-and-loop on the internal pockets only. The main compartment still closes with YKK zippers, so the change affects how you organize small items, not how the pack carries load.

Hook-and-loop is loud, collects debris and loses grip as it wears. Magnets are quiet and work with one hand, which helps if you pack up before dawn while the house is asleep. GORUCK hasn't published durability data for the magnets, so long-term retention is the detail to watch.

If your 4.0's pockets still close well, this is a convenience rather than a reason to buy a new pack.

If you're considering the 5.0 mainly for the magnetic closures, think about how often you open the internal pockets during a ruck. Most training sessions don't need fast access to small items.

The lumbar pad

The lumbar pad is the change most likely to affect how the pack carries. A flat pad bridges the hollow of the lower back. A contoured pad fills it, giving the pack a second point of contact below the shoulder blades.

Contoured lumbar pads are common on hiking packs, and the Rucker now has one. How well it fits depends on the shape of your back. A contour built around a typical lumbar curve will suit some people better than a flat pad and others less well.

Lumbar contact becomes more noticeable as the load gets heavier. If you're new to rucking, start with 5 to 10 pounds and read how heavy your ruck should be before judging any pack at heavier loads.

Does it address real problems?

The magnetic closures fix a small, common annoyance. Worn or noisy hook-and-loop is easy to understand, and magnets remove it.

The lumbar redesign is the substantive change. It is aimed at the 4.0 owner who finishes rucks with a sore or rubbed lower back, or who feels the bottom of the pack rocking. Whether it suits your back is a fit question.

The rest is familiar. The shell, frame sheet and plate pockets carry over, so apart from the lumbar contact a 5.0 carries much like a 4.0.

GORUCK's SCARS Lifetime Guarantee says it will repair or replace gear worn out or damaged through use, at GORUCK's discretion. Its guarantee FAQ lists exclusions, including washer or dryer damage, so read the current terms on GORUCK's site before relying on it.

Price and availability

The Rucker 5.0 sits in the same price band as the Rucker 4.0 and the Mystery Ranch 2 Day Assault, above GORUCK's own Basic Rucker. Ruck plates are sold separately. The product cards below show current price bands.

The premium buys a dedicated, elevated plate-pocket design, the SCARS Lifetime Guarantee and a fit with GORUCK's plate system.

Should you upgrade?

The decision depends on your current pack:

If you're on a Rucker 4.0: Keep it if it fits well. The upgrade makes sense if its lumbar pad bothers you at your usual load or its hook-and-loop pockets have worn out.

If you're on an earlier Rucker: You get the same two changes, and an older pack may also be due for replacement. Compare the 5.0's plate pockets and sizes with what you carry now.

If you're buying your first pack: You can start with a backpack you already own and a secured, measurable load. If you're still rucking regularly after a few months and want a dedicated plate pocket, the 5.0 is a reasonable long-term buy, and the Basic Rucker is GORUCK's lower-cost option.

The lumbar pad and the magnetic closures are the two changes you'd notice day to day. Whether they justify an upgrade depends on how much your current pack's lumbar pad and pockets bother you.

Frequently asked questions
