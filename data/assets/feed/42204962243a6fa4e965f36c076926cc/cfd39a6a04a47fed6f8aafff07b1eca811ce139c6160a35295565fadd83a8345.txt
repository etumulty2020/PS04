FAQ

GORUCK Ruck Plates come in Standard (9 × 11.5 in) and Long (9.5 × 15 in) formats, sized for the Rucker's plate pockets and GORUCK's carrier.

The Ruck Plate Carrier 3.0 (Standard) is a back-only harness. Its 9.75 × 11.75 × 1.5 in pocket takes one 10, 20 or 30 lb Standard plate, and it has no other storage.

A front-and-back weight vest is a different system. It splits the load between chest and back and usually takes its own curved or vest-sized plates.

Either way you need a plate. A backpack you already own costs nothing extra; a carrier is an added purchase.

Compare How Each Load Sits

Ruck plates carried in a backpack and plates worn in a carrier put the load in different places. That changes comfort, heat, movement, storage, and the kinds of training each setup suits.

The term plate carrier covers two different designs. GORUCK's Ruck Plate Carrier 3.0 is a back-only harness that holds one plate high on your back and leaves the chest open. A military-style front-and-back vest splits the load between chest and back. This guide compares both with a plate carried in a backpack.

For walking, hiking, and longer rucks, a backpack is usually the more practical choice because it carries water and layers as well as the plate. A carrier suits shorter sessions where you want the load tight to your body, and a front-and-back vest suits training that needs to resemble body armor.

This is a research-based comparison built from GORUCK's published specifications for its Ruck Plates, Ruck Plate Carrier and Rucker 5.0, checked October 9, 2026. We have not tested the carrier.

Weight Distribution Mechanics

A backpack puts the whole load behind you, so you lean slightly forward to keep it balanced over your feet. How high and how close the plate rides depends on the pocket and the shoulder straps. The Rucker 5.0 holds plates in two elevated internal pockets, and a pack with a load-bearing hip belt can move some of the weight off your shoulders.

GORUCK's Ruck Plate Carrier also loads only your back, but it holds a single plate high between the shoulder blades with nothing else in the bag. GORUCK says the open chest is there so you can breathe more freely.

A front-and-back vest splits the load between chest and back, which keeps it centered over your hips instead of behind you. The panels wrap the torso, so a tight vest can feel restrictive when you are breathing hard.

Do not assume the same weight will feel the same in all three setups. Start lighter when changing systems and judge the load by how you move and breathe, not by the number on the plate.

Comfort and Usability Factors

A purpose-made ruck plate is flat, has rounded edges and is sized for a plate pocket. GORUCK's Standard plates share a 9 × 11.5 in footprint and get thicker as they get heavier; its Long plates are 9.5 × 15 in. That shape sits flatter against the back than a round gym plate or a bag of loose weight.

Backpacks and GORUCK's carrier leave the chest open. A front-and-back vest covers the front and back of the torso, so it usually feels hotter in warm weather or during hard efforts.

Storage is where a backpack pulls ahead for longer rucks. It carries water, food and layers alongside the plate, which makes it practical for hiking and extended sessions. The Ruck Plate Carrier holds a plate and nothing else, so water and layers have to live somewhere else. Front-and-back vests vary, so check what storage a given model has.

A carrier or vest holds the load tighter to the body than most packs, so it bounces and sways less when you run, jump or change direction. That makes either one a better fit for workouts that mix walking with running or bodyweight moves.

Adjustment also differs. Many backpacks let you change torso length, shoulder fit, and load position. GORUCK's carrier comes in Standard and Long sizes, GORUCK suggests the Long if you have big traps, and it has a sternum strap but no load-bearing waist belt. Front-and-back vests often have a narrower adjustment range, so checking the maker's sizing chart matters.

Training Specificity and Goals

Match the setup to the session. For walks, hikes and anything long enough to need water, a backpack carries what you need. For a short session that mixes walking with running, lunges or other bodyweight work, a carrier keeps the plate tighter to your body.

If your job involves body armor, a front-and-back vest is the closer match to what you wear at work, including the coverage and the heat.

Over longer distances, the heat and chest coverage of a front-and-back vest add up, and a plate-only carrier leaves nowhere to put water. For long rucks, a backpack is the practical default.

Check an event's packing list before you train for it in a carrier. If the list requires water, food or other kit alongside the weight, a plate-only carrier cannot hold them, so train in the pack you will use on the day.

In any setup, add time before weight, and start lighter whenever you switch systems. Stop and lighten the load if you feel sharp pain or numbness or tingling in your hands or arms, and check with a clinician before adding weight if you have a back or joint condition.

Choose Between a Ruck Plate and Plate Carrier

Carry a plate in a backpack if you are new to rucking or want to go long: it holds water and layers and gives you the most room to adjust the fit. Add GORUCK's Ruck Plate Carrier for shorter plate-only sessions, or a front-and-back vest if you want training that resembles body armor. Whichever you choose, start lighter than your backpack load when you switch.
