Best Outdoor and Camping Blankets of 2026

Puffy Blankets, Picnic Blankets, Waterproof Blankets, and Fleece and Down Blankets for Camping, Travel, Festivals, and Cabins

October 1st, 2026, updated with four new winnersHome >Gear Reviews >Camping

Camping blankets are not just about staying cozy. They are built for the dirt, mud, spills, and damp conditions that come with spending time outside. Unlike the old sheet or bed comforter many of us used to haul along, a good outdoor blanket should be easy to clean, resistant to moisture, and compact enough that you will actually bring it.

I first encountered puffy outdoor blankets at a trade show in 2016 and have been using and evaluating them ever since. For this update, I took them on 40 flights, desert trips in Joshua Tree and the Mojave, a month-long summer camp in the Eastern Sierra with other testers, five-day fishing trips in Montana, and outdoor festivals in Oregon. We compared water resistance by suspending each blanket like a hammock, pouring half a liter of water onto it, and timing how long it took to leak through. Some held the water for more than six hours without fully wetting through.

We evaluated each blanket for warmth, water resistance, weight, packed size, durability, comfort, and ease of cleaning. We also considered whether it worked best as an insulating layer, a ground cover, or a blanket that could reasonably do both.

Top Pick: The Rumpl Original Puffy Blanket earns our Best Overall award. It stood out for its water resistance, durable recycled construction, easy machine washability, and useful balance of warmth and packability. In our spill test, it did not begin leaking through the stitching for 15 minutes and was still holding most of the water three hours later. Corner loops make it easier to secure in windy conditions, while its range of sizes and designs works for solo camping, festivals, road trips, and everyday outdoor use.

Note: This guide includes insulated blankets designed primarily for warmth, durable ground blankets for picnics and damp campsites, and versatile options that can handle both jobs.

Best Outdoor Blanket: Rumpl Original Puffy Blanket

The Rumpl Original Puffy Blanket is what you think of when you think of a puffy blanket for outdoor use. It's soft, comes in beautiful designs, is well-stitched, and comes with a convenient tote bag that makes it pack down to a small size. It works well to break the chill around a campfire (or on the couch!). It's a shareable alternative to a sleeping bag on warm days and adds extra warmth on top of a sleeping bag on cold nights. We even used it as a picnic blanket at festivals because it's so packable.

Best yet, Rumpl is one of the most eco-friendly outdoor brands and is a Climate Neutral Certified brand where 100% of the shell and insulation are post-consumer recycled materials and 100 plastic bottles are recycled for each double blanket.

There are still more color and print options with Rumpl than other brands, so we still think it's the best camping blanket.

The Rumpl Original comes in beautiful designs that support artists, like this blanket by Tlingit and Athabascan artist Crystal Worl. Photo by Kate Hoch.

The Rumpl Original Puffy blanket can be used instead of a sleeping bag on warm nights. Photo by Kate Hoch.

Updates

New for this year, the Rumpl Original is available in mini and throw sizes designed for kids or pets.

Sizes

The Rumpl Original Puffy blanket is available in three sizes—a junior, a single (75x52 inches) and a double (84×80 inches). In our experience, the single can easily be shared with two people when using it as a picnic blanket. If the couple is sharing a double camp chair like the Kelty Low, the single should be fine, too. But if you're looking to use the blanket at night instead of a sleeping bag, you'll want the double, no matter how well you know the other person!

Patterns and colors

The biggest advantage of the Rumpl over other blankets is that it has the most options, colors, patterns, and designs. Camping blankets are large and many people make a statement with them. From the RAD (Rumpl Artist Division) that supports outdoors artists to their National Park line, there are colors, patterns, and designs for everyone. We tested a blanket with a design by Crystal Worl, a Tlingit and Athabascan artist.

Cape clip

Another less obvious perk of the Rumpl blanket over other puffy blankets is the Cape Clip, which allows you to turn it into a cape for walking around camp. You can even use it as a changing poncho in a pinch, though we used it for a month at the camp's outdoor shower and it required getting creative.

The Rumpl is one of the few blankets we tested that has corner loops to stake out your blanket on windy days.

Warmth

The Rumpl Original sits in the middle of the pack in terms of warmth. It isn't the warmest blanket, but it's noticeably warmer than others we tested, including the similar looking Cascade Mountain Tech. We used it as an alternative to a sleeping bag while camping by hot springs in the Eastern Sierra in the peak of summer and didn't find it warm enough for a night in the 40s. But we found it perfect for sitting around a campfire, sitting on the porch of a cabin for sunset, or watching an outdoor movie.

Water-resistance

The Rumpl Original is also among the more water-resistant camp blankets we tested. When we dumped a 0.5 L Nalgene bottle on it, it took 15 minutes to start to leak through, and that was only at the stitching. We cut the experiment off after 3 hours, but even then, it was still holding most of the water. Since it has synthetic insulation, the Rumpl stays warm, even when it's misty or wet, unlike down blankets.

Easy washing

The Original Puffy Blanket is made of easy-to-wash synthetic insulation that can be thrown in the washing machine, which is handy since we always used our blanket around the campfire and wanted to remove the smoke odor.

For its balance of warmth, price, sustainability, ease of washing, packability, and cool patterns and designs, the Rumpl Original is an easy overall winner for best camping blanket.

The Nomadix Festival Blanket is our winner for best picnic blanket. It's so waterproof, it never wetted out or leaked in over 3 hours of testing supporting half a liter of water. Slip-resistant

The Nomadix is slip resistant, which it claims makes it good for hot yoga. To test this claim, we took it to our studio. While this blanket isn’t a typical hot yoga mat or hot yoga towel and I felt a little weird doing it, the Nomadix blanket stayed grippy even when wet. That grippiness also proves useful outdoors, such as when you're using it as a beach blanket if your feet are still wet coming out of the water.

Stain-resistant

The backside of this blanket is black, so it doesn’t show stains where it is against the ground. In our testing, sand and dirt didn't stick as much to the bottom material as the top material. The top side (where others will see it) comes in fun prints. We wish there were more colors, though.

Sustainability

The Nomadix Festival Blanket is made of Global Recycled Standard-certified post-consumer recycled plastic. The Global Recycled Standard (GRS) considered environmental impacts as well as social impacts like conditions for workers.

Comparison to other picnic blankets

The Festival Blanket and Rumpl Everywhere Mat (review below) have many of the same features including corner loops. Main differences we found were in size, weight (32 oz vs 19 oz) and price ($69 vs $80). The Festival Blanket is less expensive and bigger, which is why we recommend it. However, it does not stuff into its own pocket, a feature you can find in the Everywhere Mat.

We liked the Festival Blanket's size better for gatherings with more than three people. We liked the Festival Blanket better for using as a porch with 6-person tents.

My biggest issue with the Festival Blanket is I wish it had more color and design options (admittedly, the Rumpl Blanket has about the same offerings). The price is steep as well. Still, its waterproofness is impressive and I have a hard time imagining buying this for myself, but it's the sort of item I would be thrilled to receive as a gift.

The Backcountry Puffy is designed to be more packable and weather-resistant than Rumpl's previous models of warm blankets, including their (now discontinued) Down Blanket.

Waterproofing

In our waterproof testing, the Rumpl Backcountry kept 0.5 L of water from coming through for more than 6 hours (we stopped the test before it ever broke through).

Cape clip to wear it

As with other Rumpl Blankets, the Backcountry Puffy comes with the Cape Clip to make it wearable as well as Corner Staking loops in case you want to turn it into a picnic blanket.

Excellent dry bag included

You could easily attach it to a backpack with a carabiner or lashing if you wanted extra warmth. It also has a zip pocket in the corner so you don’t lose the stuff sack when in motion.

Sustainability

The Backcountry Blanket is made of post-consumer recycled content and the brand is a B-Corp that is Certified Climate Neutral and also a member of 1% for the Planet.

It’s even kind of fashionable. The downside is this blanket is expensive, but with quality sleeping bag-grade materials, I expect this poncho to last as long as a down sleeping bag, which means 10 years or more.

For campers and backpackers who want the warmth of a sleeping bag without the restrictiveness (or awkwardness) of a mummy bag, the made in the USA Western Mountaineering Cloud 9 is the best camp blanket for cold weather.

The Western Mountaineering Cloud 9 is a luxurious and thick puffy blanket that packs down to the size of a Nalgene bottle. Its rectangular shape is available in twin, queen, and king sizes–to reflect that it is trying to be the anti-mummy bag–and is completely non-constricting just like your bed at home.

I've been using my Cloud 9 comforter on my bed at home for almost a decade because I like how fluffy and comfortable it feels. It's also a great addition to bring on cold plane and train trips because it is so compact.

Insulation

The Western Mountaineering Cloud 9 Comforter is stuffed with 800-fill European goose down, which is the same insulation found in Western Mountaineering's high-end sleeping bags. Each square sewn through baffle is as puffy as a cloud. It's a joy to snuggle with and does its job of being an extremely effective insulator. You can read about down fill and how down works for insulation in our guide to Best Down Jackets or Best Sleeping Bags.

Warmth

The Western Mountaineering Cloud 9 Comforter is designed to be an alternative to a down sleeping bag. It is made of the same high-end materials you'd find in a Western Mountaineering down sleeping bag, but with a rectangular shape. This makes it excellent as a camping sleeping bag or as an alternative to a backpacking quilt.

While camping in Oregon, I found its size and shape worked particularly well with a camping cot. It was much easier to work around the edges compared to the sleeping bag I also brought (and subsequently pushed to the side).

Straps

The Western Mountaineering Cloud Comfort has straps on each corner so you can tie it closer to your body or secure it to your sleeping pad or even to use in a camping hammock. However, this attachment system relies on your ability to tie knots. In our testing for our backpacking quilts guide, we've found far more sophisticated and easy-to-use systems. While we appreciate the option of the tie-outs and they're better than nothing, we find them too analog to want to use except in the colder conditions.

Washing

Like all down blankets, the Western Mountaineering Cloud 9 is not the easiest to wash. It's also quite expensive. But if you're looking for a down comforter for camping that you can also use for home, it's the best blanket.

Our verdict

If you're looking for a warm and compact blanket to use for travel, we also think the Western Mountaineering is the best option. It packs down to a 6"x10" size stuff sack (4.5L). It isn't the lightest blanket at 25 oz, but it is not even in the same league of warmth as the lighter-weight blankets we tested. 25 oz is excellent for a down sleeping bag or a backpacking quilt and that is the level of warmth you'll get with this bag.

The Sackcloth and Ashes outdoor blanket receives rave reviews and we decided to test it based on a Treeline reader's recommendation. This is a beautiful blanket that you'd expect to see on some outdoor Instagram influencer's account as they sit atop a mountain looking at sunset.

But it turns out, looking good doesn't mean something isn't functional. After almost 3 years of testing the Sackcloth and Ashes, we found even more to love about it even beyond its attractive style.

It's the softest blanket we tested. The Sackcloth and Ashes is warm, it's water resistant, it's eco-friendly and the brand donates to shelters for people experiencing houselessness. And unlike most wool blankets, it's not itchy and is actually washable.

So yes, the Sackcloth and Ashes blanket shows it is possible to look good and be a functional outdoor blanket, too.

Style

There's no ignoring the Sackcloth and Ashes outdoor blanket's style. For context, you can get this blanket at Saks Fifth Avenue. Still, the price is reasonable, especially considering that it is made in Italy and finished in the US and a blanket is donated to a homeless shelter for every one you purchase.

Ethical and sustainable

As mentioned above, Sackcloth and Ashes donates a blanket to a homeless shelter for every blanket that is purchased. The material itself is made of recycled acrylic and recycled polyester. The wool is made from remnants from other factory floors to divert it from the landfill. The brand says each blanket is made of 75-100% recycled materials.

The brand says their blankets are "intentionally and responsibly made," and this seems to be one of the better examples I've seen in the outdoor industry.

Machine-washable materials

Ok, this is where I'm putting a "stretch" to the award of Best Wool Blanket. This blanket has only 20% wool. Normally, I would ding myself for that error, but this blanket looks like a traditional wool blanket. And by not having more wool, it means you can actually wash it in a washing machine. It also isn't itchy like wool.

Our verdict

For the beauty of patterns (including designs by indigenous artists), eco-friendly materials, and ethical donation of blankets–as well as the functionality of this blanket, we think it's worth getting if you want something that looks and feels luxurious that you can feel good about purchasing.

Best Emergency Camp Blanket: SOL Emergency Blanket

The SOL Emergency Blanket is the best outdoor blanket to keep around for emergencies. It’s small, lightweight, and compact, and is designed for situations where you really don’t want to carry a sleeping bag but should probably have a source of warmth, just in case.

Warmth

I tested the warmth capabilities of the SOL Emergency Blanket on an ill-fated alpine climb in Yosemite’s high country where we summitted at sunset and then had to rappel slowly down in the dark. What makes it better

All emergency blankets are fairly similar and the technology hasn't changed significantly since the early days. One advantage of the SOL over other blankets are its dimensions and that it is made of recycled material.

Still, I carry one with me on almost all my winter day hikes in snowy conditions because I have seen how much they really do work when you need it.

We also liked how the SOL emergency blanket is available in an XL size as well as in 4-packs. This just makes emergency blankets more useful for bigger bodies and for families.

What To Look For In An Outdoor Blanket

Material

Camping blankets are available in many materials–from synthetic shells to fleece to wool. To decide which material is best for you, think about how you're going to use your camping blanket. If it's going to get wet, you will likely want something that dries quickly. If it's going to be used on the ground, on the grass, or in the mud, you likely want a material that washes easily. Lastly, how do you want the blanket to feel against your skin? If you have an allergy to wool, for example, that's likely not the material you want.

Durability

The camping blankets we tested have different levels of durability depending on how they're supposed to be used. Picnic blankets and beach blankets, for example, are designed to be used on the ground, in the mud, or on uneven surfaces. As a result, they're abrasion resistant and puncture resistant so they can last a long time.

On the other hand, insulated puffy blankets have some level of durability on the ground, but ultimately are best used when layered on top of a person for insulation instead of against the ground. A good camping blanket should be able to last a decade of use without needing replacing.

Size

Some camp blankets are available in single, double, or even bigger sizes. Depending on how you're using the blanket and how many people are in your family (or typical camping group), you'll want to size accordingly.

Weight

If you plan to backpack with your camping blanket or are using your blanket at a beach or festival where parking is far away, weight will be a concern. Think about how you'll use your camping blanket and how much weight matters to you. Lighter-weight camping blankets tend to be either more expensive (like the Western Mountaineering Comforter) or less warm because they have less insulation.

Portability

One of the major differences between a camping blanket and a blanket on your bed at home is portability. Most camping blankets are made of packable materials and insulation that compresses well like down. Most camp blankets we tested have either a stuff sack or pocket that they stuff into to make carrying them easier.

Weather Resistance

Sometimes, your picnic or camping trip is going to be in the rain. While camping blankets usually aren't designed for use in precipitation, if it's a light rain or mist that is intermittent, many blankets come treated with DWR (Durable Water Repellent). This is enough to have water droplets bead up and roll off so you can pay attention to your campfire or the outdoor music festival or movie.

For blankets that are designed for use under you–like picnic blankets and beach blankets–you want the blanket to resist mud or water that might be under you. Look for a waterproof blanket that keeps water out on one side, but feels soft on the other side (the side you'll be on!)

Insulation And Warmth

I'd argue that the most important feature of most outdoor blankets is keeping you warm. However, warmth is a balance between price, packability, and weight. Think about what season and where you'll be using your camping blanket. What are the average temperatures? Will you be using it overnight? We rank each blanket according to warmth so you can choose the blanket according to your needs. Pair a camping blanket with a fleece jacket for the ultimate in coziness.

Machine Washable Vs Hand Wash

For blankets that you sit on, like picnic blankets and beach blankets, machine washable is a must since they are likely to encounter dirt more frequently than other camp blankets. Even for camping blankets, machine washable is nice since chances are you'll be getting campfire smoke on the blanket with some regularity.

Stain Resistance

When you're outdoors, stains happen to your gear. Whether it's mud or grass stains or accidentally dropping the s'mores on the way from the campfire to your mouth (oops!), I've developed a number of stains on my camp blankets during testing. The best camp blankets have some Durable Water Repellent(DWR) treatment that prevents some of the staining, though in my experience, that doesn't seem to be quite enough for all types of stains.

Outdoor Blanket Care And Maintenance

Always check the care label before washing an outdoor blanket. Puffy blankets, down comforters, fleece-lined blankets, and waterproof-backed picnic blankets may all require different treatment.

Shake off dirt, sand, pine needles, and other debris after each trip. Small spills are often easier to wipe away or spot-clean before they soak into the fabric, so you won't need a full wash every time.

Most machine-washable synthetic blankets can be cleaned with cold water, mild detergent, and a gentle cycle. A front-loading washer is the safer choice for puffy blankets, since a central agitator can catch or damage lightweight shell fabric. Avoid bleach, fabric softener, and harsh stain treatments unless the manufacturer specifically permits them.

Down blankets need more care. Use the washing and drying method listed by the manufacturer, and make sure the down dries completely so the fill doesn't clump or develop mildew. Our guide to washing a down sleeping bag has more detailed instructions for down-filled outdoor gear.

Dry every blanket fully before packing it away. Some can go in a dryer on low heat, while others should be air-dried, so follow the label rather than using one method for every product. Store insulated blankets loosely when possible instead of leaving them compressed in their stuff sacks for long periods.

Check the shell, stitching, corner loops, and waterproof backing for damage before storing the blanket. Small holes in puffy fabrics are usually easier to repair with a proper gear patch before they spread or insulation starts escaping.

Ways You Can Use Outdoor Blankets

We used camp blankets while camping, staying at cabins, at outdoor festivals, and outdoor movies and everything in between.

Outdoor blankets earn their keep well beyond the campfire. Once you have a good one, you start finding reasons to bring it everywhere.

Camping. Beyond warmth, a blanket adds a layer between your sleeping bag or pad and the ground, which is especially welcome on colder nights.

Pets. A blanket gives dogs and adventure cats a designated, cozy spot to settle in your tent or van rather than curling up directly on cold ground or gear.

Seating. Spread out, a blanket creates an instant gathering spot around a campfire or campsite, giving you a soft, insulated place to sit and socialize instead of the bare ground.

We tested outdoor blankets side-by-side while on camping trips in peak summer as well as in cooler conditions.

Style. With the range of colors and designs available now, a blanket can be as much a style statement as a functional piece of gear.

Protection. A blanket is handy for protecting things beyond yourself, too. Toss it over car seats after a muddy hike, or wrap it around something fragile to keep it from bouncing around on a rough drive.

The more you use an outdoor blanket, the more uses you find for it. It's the kind of gear that starts as a camping essential and ends up living in the car, on the couch, and in the trunk for good.

FAQ

What is the difference between a camping blanket and a regular blanket?

Camping blankets are specifically built for outdoor conditions that household blankets are not. They repel water and mud, dry quickly, resist odors, shake off sand and debris more easily, and pack down to a fraction of their size for transport. Most are also easier to machine wash than standard comforters or throws, which makes them genuinely more practical for camping, festivals, and travel even though they cost more upfront.

How warm are camping blankets?

Warmth varies widely depending on insulation weight, fill type, and blanket size. None of the camping blankets we tested carry standardized temperature ratings like sleeping bags do, so comparisons between brands rely on fill weight and real-world testing. As a general guide, lighter blankets under 20 oz work well for campfire sitting and mild summer nights, while heavier insulated options like the Rumpl Backcountry Blanket can handle cold ridgelines and frosty desert mornings.

Is down or synthetic insulation better for a camping blanket?

Down offers the best warmth-to-weight ratio and compresses smaller than synthetic fill, but it loses insulating power when wet and is far more difficult to wash, often requiring specialty detergent and hours of drying time. Synthetic insulation stays warm even when damp, machine washes easily, and costs less, but is bulkier for the same warmth. For blankets that will see regular outdoor use and washing, synthetic is the more practical choice. For travel or home use where packability matters most and washing is infrequent, down is worth the tradeoff.

Are camping blankets waterproof?

Waterproofness varies significantly by type. Puffy camping blankets with DWR-treated shells repel light moisture and beaded water but will eventually soak through. Purpose-built ground blankets and picnic blankets, like the Nomadix Festival Blanket, are closer to fully waterproof and are designed to sit on wet grass or damp ground. In our testing, the best picnic blankets held 0.5 liters of water for over three hours without leaking through.

Are camping blankets safe near a campfire?

Most camping blankets are not fireproof and can melt, scorch, or develop burn holes from stray embers. Standard synthetic and down shells are particularly vulnerable. The exception is the Rumpl NanoLoft Flame Resistant Blanket, which uses a modacrylic and cotton shell specifically designed to resist sparks and flame contact, making it the only blanket in this guide we would confidently recommend for sitting close to an open fire.

Can a camping blanket replace a sleeping bag?

Generally not in cold or unpredictable conditions. A camping blanket works well as a standalone sleep layer on warm summer nights or as extra warmth layered on top of a sleeping bag when temperatures drop. Unlike a sleeping bag, a blanket does not seal drafts along the sides and provides no insulation beneath your body, where heat loss to the ground is significant. For use below 50 degrees, pair it with a sleeping bag or a high-R-value sleeping pad.

What is the difference between a camping blanket and a backpacking quilt?

A backpacking quilt is designed specifically as a sleep system component, with sewn baffles optimized for consistent loft, often a footbox or snap closures to seal out drafts, and attachment points to secure it to a sleeping pad. A camping blanket is a more general-purpose rectangle without those sleep-specific features. Quilts are generally warmer per ounce and better suited as a primary sleep layer, while camping blankets are more versatile for sitting, lounging, and layering but less efficient as a standalone sleep system in cold conditions.

Can you use a puffy camping blanket on the ground?

You can, but most puffy camping blankets are better suited for use over your body than under it. The thin ripstop nylon shells on most puffy blankets can snag, puncture, or abrade against rough ground surfaces, and many are not waterproof enough to block moisture from damp grass or soil. If you need a blanket that works on the ground, a dedicated picnic blanket or waterproof ground mat is the more durable and practical choice.
