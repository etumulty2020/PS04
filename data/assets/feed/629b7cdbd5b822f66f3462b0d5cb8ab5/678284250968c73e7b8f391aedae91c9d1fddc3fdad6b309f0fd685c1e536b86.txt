Pad pumps for inflatable sleeping pads for backpacking and camping mattresses

For ultralight backpackers, thru-hikers, and backpackers of all types, inflating a sleeping pad is the worst camp chore. It either requires huffing and puffing or capturing air in a stuff sack and pumping it, which is significantly less fun to do if it is windy, buggy, cold, rainy, or you're at altitude. With 3.5" and 4" inflatable backpacking pads becoming the trend, there is more inflating needed than ever. Pad pumps, aka sleeping pad inflators, use batteries and fans to inflate your sleeping pad, eliminating one of the worst camp chores.

Electric pad inflators are USB-powered or battery-powered, and are faster and require less human power and attention than manual pumps, pump sacks that come with your pad, or breathing into your pad. Pad pumps are often lighter than manual pump sacks, too. This guide doesn't cover manual sleeping pad inflators, which I find annoying and way less convenient than electric pad inflators.

We spent 1.5 years testing as many sleeping pad inflators as we could find, from those that work for every backpacking pad or camping mattress out there to ones that only work for one manufacturer. We tested while backpacking on the Arizona Trail and off-trail on the Grand Enchantment Trail, as well as while backpacking in Northern California, the Washington Coast, the Eastern Sierra, Colorado, and while thru-hiking the West Coast Trail. We also ran side-by-side lab tests, noting inflation time on the most popular standard test pads, battery drain, valve compatibility, and noise measured by decibels.

To judge pad pumps, we considered weight, inflation speed, battery life, power sources, valve compatibility, noise level, durability, and value. We covered everything from sub-30g USB fans to 100g+ multi-function electric vacuum pumps. Ultimately, the right pick depends heavily on what pad you have and how much you value weight vs. speed.

What to look for in sleeping pad inflators

USB-powered vs. self-contained electric vs. manual

Sleeping pad inflators work several ways: battery-powered or manual. We didn't consider any manual pumps or pumpsacks in this guide (but if you are wondering, the best pumpsack out there for a backpacking pad is the one made by Sea to Summit. Alas, it only works with Sea to Summit pads.)

USB-powered inflators work by connecting your phone or power bank via a USB cord to the inflator.

Self-contained electric inflators have a small lithium battery inside the inflator. You charge the whole unit or just the battery separately. No power bank or phone battery drainage required.

Weight and system weight

For a backpacking pad inflator, weight is likely one of your most important criteria. Consider the weight of the unit and its adapters (and included cords and batteries). If you're on a shorter backpacking trip, you may not be carrying a power bank. So if you're just carrying a power bank for your pad inflator, that is a lot of extra weight to add to the equation.

For a car camping mattress inflator, weight will matter less. In fact, we recommend the heavier-duty inflators for car camping. Inflating camping mattresses manually is super annoying, and we think it is worth getting the camping mattress inflator whose battery will never die so you aren't stuck manually pumping.

Inflation speed

Different backpackers and campers value inflation speed differently. It depends on how patient of a person you are. For backpacking, I think getting a slower inflation speed is fine because I'll set up my pad pump and then do other camp chores while I am waiting for it. One of my backpacking partners likes to stare at the pad while it is inflating and cannot multitask, so speed matters a lot more to him. Figure out your camp chore style and choose accordingly.

For what it is worth, many of these inflators can be plugged in and left to do their thing. The pump will usually stall or stop pushing air before the pad bursts. But check with your specific pad inflators' instructions before deciding to step away from your pad whilst inflating.

Battery life and power consumption

We tested how much battery life a pad pump uses based on power consumption to inflate a standard pad and run-time (for self-contained units). In most cases, you can measure the battery life on pumps in terms of runtime or the number of inflations. That being said, some pad inflators use more battery than others, so if you are on a longer trip far away from power outlets or solar panels, you may want to opt for a model that has lower battery needs. For thru-hikers resupplying in town, some models only require you to charge the same battery pack you're likely already using for your phone, headlamps, and other electronics. Other models require you to charge another separate internal battery designed just for your pad inflator. If you're carrying a model with an external battery, you may benefit from carrying a wall charger with an extra USB port.

Noise

Some pad pumps we tested are so loud, we don't think they'd pass muster to run past quiet hours at a campground. Others, we joked, violate Leave No Trace's Principle to Let Nature's Sounds Prevail. We used the Decibel X app to test the decibel level of different pumps.

Deflate function

Not all pad inflators have deflate functions, but when they do, it makes breaking down camp a lot easier.

Durability

Backpacking pad inflators have small fans that must be protected from sand, grit, and dirt. USB ports need to be kept free of debris. Camping pad pumps need to survive getting tossed around in gear boxes. None of the pumps broke, but the plastic battery cover on the NeoAir inflator detached and we were hypervigilant about getting sand in Alpenglow pad fans. We kept durability in mind when considering pad inflators.

Do you actually need a sleeping pad inflator?

Nope. For backpacking pads, most include a pump sack or can be inflated by breath (though that can introduce moisture and potentially mold to the inside of your pad).

However, a pad pump is life-changing. I generally hate pump sacks. I find them heavy, difficult to justify as a multi-use item, with finicky valves that never seem to stick. They also don't deflate your pad.

I typically inflate by breath to save weight compared to carrying a pump sack, but it's annoying to do, especially at altitude after an exhausting day of hiking. I've lied to myself, saying the process of doing all that breathing gets fire in my belly to warm up before going to sleep. But I hate it. Plus, backpacking pads are getting thicker these days, with many top pads at nearly 4" tall! The pad pump changes everything and makes one of the worst camp chores into an easy part of an automated pre-sleep routine.

For car camping mattresses, I think a pad inflator is essential. If you're spending camping mattress dollars for a good night of sleep, I think the extra ~$60 to get a pad inflator is more than worth it. Camping has enough chores as it is. Spending 30 minutes taking turns manually inflating a camping mattress is the sort of camp chore that teaches kids that camping is not fun and makes adults never want to camp again. Get an easy-to-use, easy-to-connect mattress pump, and it is a total camping game changer.

Care and maintenance

Keep your pad inflator away from sand and dirt: That can get caught in motors and break the whole system. I keep mine in a small DCF ditty bag along with my electronics. I usually throw down a polycro or Tyvek groundsheet if I'm inflating my pad while cowboy camping. Inside a tent with a floor, I haven't taken extra precautions.

Store inflators dry: Unless the pump you choose is water-resistant, moisture inside the motor shortens lifespan.

Keep nozzle adapters in a small zip bag: They are easy to lose.

USB-powered inflators: As with all other electronics, keep the USB-C port clear of debris. Blow it out before connecting.

Electric inflators with onboard batteries: Charge before each trip (trust me on this one). Don't store fully depleted.

FAQ

Do I need a sleeping pad inflator for backpacking?

No, but it turns what’s arguably the worst camp chore into an easy task. As pads get thicker, pump sacks take longer to use. Pad pumps are often lighter than pump sacks, too. Plus, they help prevent breathlessness at altitude.

What's the difference between a USB-powered inflator and one with an onboard battery?

USB-powered inflators hook up to your phone or battery pack to run. Onboard batteries are a fully contained unit. USB-powered inflators will drain batteries that you may want to use to charge other electronics (and can drain your phone). Most onboard batteries only power the pump, but some models, like the EXPED Widget, can also charge other electronics.

How long does it take to inflate a sleeping pad with an electric inflator?

Depending on the pad pump, between 1 and 3 minutes for a standard, regular-width, 6-foot-long, 3.5" thick backpacking sleeping pad.

Will a sleeping pad inflator work at altitude?

Yes, we've tested these inflators extensively at 8,000 to 10,000 feet and have noticed no difference in inflation and deflation time compared to sea level.

Why shouldn't I just blow up my sleeping pad with my mouth?

Inflating your sleeping pad with your mouth introduces moisture and the potential for mold into the inside of your pad. It also takes a lot of breath, which is tiring and tedious at altitude after a long day of hiking.

How many times can I inflate my pad on one battery charge?

It depends on the pad pump, but for self-contained battery pad pumps, you can get about 30 inflates (or 15 inflates, 15 deflates)—give or take—off one charge.

Are sleeping pad inflators compatible with all sleeping pads?

Most sleeping pad inflators come with adapters for the most common sleeping pad valves, with options to buy adapters for less common pad valves. Check the pad inflator manufacturer’s website if you aren't sure.

Can I use a sleeping pad inflator for car camping mattresses?

It depends on the camping mattress, the required valve adapters, your patience, and the inflator’s battery life. The most minimal pad inflators are designed for backpacking pads and will be slow on most car camping mattresses. Unless you don’t mind the added wait time, you're better off with a dedicated camping mattress pad inflator.

How noisy are electric sleeping pad inflators?

In our tests, the backpacking pad inflators we tested were around 40 decibels (except for the now-discontinued Rex Creations). The camping mattress inflators are louder.

What's the lightest sleeping pad inflator available?

The Alpenglow Gear Alpenblow Classic is the lightest sleeping pad inflator we tested, at 0.3 oz.
