1UP USA Heavy Duty Hitch Bike Rack Review: Built for Rough Roads

Rock-solid stability and a bombproof build, with a few key upgrades sold separately

October 12, 2026Home → Gear Reviews → Bike Gear

Our verdict

The 1UP USA Heavy Duty rack is a stable and durable tray-style hitch rack for bikes. The all-metal construction and solid build quality make this one of the most reliable bike racks for vehicles out there, and it’s one of the most stable racks in our test.

The base model of this bike rack is fairly simple overall. If you want extra features beyond the absolute basics, like one-handed loading, an easy-access lever for tilting the rack, and integrated locks, you’ll need to tack all that onto the purchase for an additional cost.

At the base price, this is a very sturdy, durable, and simple bike rack that is also fairly lightweight and easy to stow, all at a reasonable price. With the add-ons, this rack is comparable to other premium bike racks we’ve tested in both feature set and price.

We tested the Heavy Duty 2-bike rack with the one-handed upgrade kit, the EZ Pull handle, and 1UP integrated wheel locks. We tested the rack around central Colorado on the back of an F-150, from trailheads to highways and everything in between.

1UP USA Heavy Duty Hitch Bike Rack

This is also one of the lightest racks in our test, and the easiest to assemble. Without any upgrades, the rack comes fully assembled in the box and ready for installation. It is also very low-profile and sits close to the vehicle, so it does not add too much extra length (we tested this on a full-size, four-door truck with a 6.5-foot bed, where we really appreciated this).

While there are some quirks that make it less user-friendly than some other bike racks we tested, this is one of the most stable and clearly durable racks of the bunch, and those are definitely its standout features. If you want a rack that can handle the roughest roads and stand up to hard use, the Heavy Duty won’t flinch.

How we tested

We tested the 1UP USA Heavy Duty rack over a month of consistent use on the back of a Ford F-150 truck, carrying two full-suspension mountain bikes, each weighing about 30 pounds. We drove to trailheads around Summit County, Colorado, often on rough dirt roads, to test the rack’s stability and all-around usefulness. We also took the rack on a road trip in Colorado, where we encountered both dirt roads and high-speed highway drives in wind and rain.

We often tilted the rack, with or without bikes loaded, to access the truck bed. In addition to all the standard testing, we also had a toddler who repeatedly crawled and climbed on the rack, playing with multiple parts, and despite his best efforts, he was unable to break or damage any part of it.

Performance in the field

Loading ease

We tested the 1UP USA Heavy Duty rack with the one-handed upgrade, which makes loading easier. This makes the ratcheting cam adjustment on the cradle arms easier to move, adjust, and secure, which in turn makes it much easier for one person to load the rack.

Because this is a low-profile rack that doesn’t sit very high, it’s fairly easy to load overall. But it’s worth noting that, with just the base model, tilting the rack up and down requires you to reach under the rack (without the EZ Pull add-on), and tightening and adjusting the cradle arms takes more time and both hands than with the more expensive one-handed upgrade option.

Bike security and stability

This is one of the most stable racks we’ve tested. Even on very rough roads or at high speeds with wind, this rack and the bikes on it don’t budge. The anti-wobble integration in the hitch helps with this, as does the rack's overall stable aluminum construction.

As for bike security, besides a hitch lock that secures the rack to the car, all of this must be purchased separately or as an add-on; wheel locks and a cable lock are available, but they are not included with the base model. We tested the rack with 1UP wheel locks and found them easy to use, reliable, and secure enough to leave our bikes unattended in town on the car.

Rear-vehicle access

The rack tilts into four positions: upright and stowed, tilted downward, a flat loading position, and one slightly tilted position in between. When tilted down to the farthest position, we were able to open the truck's tailgate so it could sit flat, allowing access to the bed, and this worked with and without bikes loaded. This same tilt position would also allow plenty of space for an SUV's hatch to open.

We used the EZ Pull handle upgrade/add-on in testing. This made it much easier to lower and tilt the rack, given that without it you have to reach under the rack for a more finicky adjustment, though it will still tilt without the EZ Pull. If you’re considering this rack, we recommend this upgrade for greater ease of use.

But we did notice that, compared to other racks we tested, the EZ Pull handle was still not the easiest to use or engage. It can be hard to pull up to tilt the rack initially and is not as easy to engage as the foot or hand levers on other models (like the super user-friendly Kuat Piston Pro).

Bike-fit versatility

We tested this rack primarily with mountain bikes: one with 27.5” wheels and one with 29” wheels. It has a fairly wide range of tire compatibility, fitting wheels with tires as small as 16” and up to 29”, meaning it can fit most bikes, including kids’ bikes and mountain bikes. The base model rack can fit tires up to 3” wide; for tires larger than that (up to 5”), you need to purchase a spacer kit. We did not test the rack with fat bikes or the spacer kit.

E-bike capability

The 2-bike version of the Heavy Duty can carry bikes weighing up to 65 pounds per tray, including e-bikes. A loading ramp is sold separately. While we did not test this rack with e-bikes specifically, we were very impressed with its overall stability and think it would be a great option for folks with e-bikes.

Build quality

The 1UP USA Heavy Duty is constructed entirely of aluminum (while most other racks include a variety of plastic or silicone parts), and in our testing, we found it to be incredibly durable and reliable. It’s made in the USA, which we also appreciate. Of all the racks we tested, we found the Heavy Duty to be the simplest and most durable, with the fewest failure points overall. This is an excellent rack for folks who seek durability above all else.

Installation and hitch fit

This was the easiest rack in our test to assemble and set up. The actual rack arrived fully assembled, and all we had to do was pop it onto the hitch. The accessories (like the EZ Pull handle) had to be installed, but it was quick and easy. It also helps that this is one of the lightest racks we tested, making setup even easier overall. Total setup time was only around 15 minutes, and would be even less without any add-ons.

Stowed footprint

This is one of the lightest and lowest-profile racks we tested. It sits close to the vehicle and does not stick out very far, nor does it stand tall when stored upright on the vehicle. It also simply has a small footprint overall, so if you are worried about off-vehicle storage space for your bike rack, this would make a good choice, with stowed dimensions of about 38” x 14” x 11”.

FAQ

Does the 1UP USA Heavy Duty fit a 1.25" hitch?

No, the Heavy Duty rack is not available in a 1.25” version. It is only available in a 2” and 2.5” version.

Can the 1UP USA Heavy Duty carry e-bikes / what's the weight limit?

Yes, the Heavy Duty can carry e-bikes. The weight limit for the 2-bike version is 65 pounds per bike.

Will the 1UP USA Heavy Duty damage a carbon frame?

No, the Heavy Duty is designed not to damage carbon frames. It mounts with completely frameless contact via the tires/wheels.

Can I access my trunk/hatch with bikes loaded on the 1UP USA Heavy Duty?

Yes, this rack allows for rear vehicle access. The rack tilts, with or without bikes loaded, to allow a hatch or tailgate to open and sit flat.

Does the 1UP USA Heavy Duty fit fat tires / kids' bikes / step-through frames?

The Heavy Duty fits bikes with 16”-29” tires, including many kids’ bikes, and can accommodate tires up to 3” wide and step-through frames. If the spacer kit is purchased separately, the rack can fit tires up to 5” wide.

Can I expand the 1UP USA Heavy Duty to carry more bikes?

Yes, the 2-bike version can expand to 3 or 4 bikes. The 1-bike version can expand to support up to 3 bikes.

Similar bike racks

1UP USA Heavy Duty vs. Kuat Piston Pro

Rack type: Hitch trayBike capacity: 2, 3, or 4Hitch receiver size: 2” or 1.25”Max weight per bike: 67 lb (with 2” hitch)Total weight capacity: 134 lb (with 2” hitch)

The Kuat Piston Pro is one of the most premium bike racks we tested. With all the upgrades we tested on the 1UP USA Heavy Duty, these racks have similar features at a similar price, though the base model Heavy Duty is several hundred dollars more affordable than the Piston Pro.

Both racks have a similar mounting style. The most notable difference between these two racks is that the Piston Pro has piston arms that make it easy to move, adjust, and secure the cradle arms to the bike tires. The Piston Pro is hands-down the most user-friendly rack we tested, with the hand/foot lever also being much easier to engage than the EZ Pull handle upgrade on the Heavy Duty rack (and far easier and quicker than adjusting or tilting the Heavy Duty without that upgrade).

Both of these racks offer similar stability overall; they’re very solid. The Piston Pro is easier to load and more user-friendly overall, but the pistons require a little more upkeep. The Heavy Duty has fewer complex moving parts and is easier to care for.

If you care most about simplicity and durability, consider the Heavy Duty. But if you want a very user-friendly rack with loads of features, the Piston Pro may be a better fit.

1UP USA Heavy Duty vs. Hollywood Racks CrossTrack

Rack type: Hitch trayBike capacity: 1 or 2 (tested)Hitch receiver size: 2” (tested) or 1.25”Max weight per bike: 66 lbTotal weight capacity: 132 lb

The Hollywood Racks CrossTrack has a very similar design overall to the 1UP USA Heavy Duty, with frameless contact via the cradle arms. The cradle arms are adjustable for wheel sizes from 20” to 29” (a slightly smaller range than the Heavy Duty) and fit tires up to 4” wide (wider than the Heavy Duty’s 3” without the spacer kit).

The CrossTrack is a much more affordable option when you factor in upgrades and add-ons for the Heavy Duty that would give it the same or similar feature set to the base model CrossTrack. This makes it a great choice for riders looking for lots of functionality at a lower price. But the Heavy Duty is a more stable and durable rack overall, and is much better suited to hard use and travel on very rough dirt roads.

If you plan to do a lot of travel on rocky or rutted-out dirt roads, the Heavy Duty may be worth the splurge. But if you’re simply looking for a user-friendly tray rack on a budget, the CrossTrack is worth your consideration.
