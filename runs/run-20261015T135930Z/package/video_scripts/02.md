## Scene 2
Visual: Close-up of a riflescope turret being clicked, then a simple graphic of a box-test target.
On-screen text: Source: AccurateShooter, Oct 15 | Leupold 6.5-20X50 M1: about 5% error
Narration: Start with the clearest miss. AccurateShooter published a reader's box test of his scope's click values. He reported that his Leupold 6.5 to 20 by 50 M1 was calibrated to what's called Shooter's M-O-A, not true minute of angle. That's an error of about five percent. But he also said his Leupold 3.5 to 10 M3LR was dead on. And he wrote that lots of scopes have click values significantly off what the manufacturer advertised.

## Scene 3
Visual: Quote card with a long-range target and a drifting bullet-path graphic.
On-screen text: Bryan Litz, Applied Ballistics: "more than 50% of the time" | Opinion, no data shown
Narration: The article also quotes Bryan Litz of Applied Ballistics. In his experience, more than half the time, long-range trajectory error is actually scope adjustment error. That's an expert opinion, and the excerpt shows no data behind it. Put together with the box test, our take is simple. Click values are a spec to verify, not to trust.

## Scene 4
Visual: Sleeping bag in a tent at dawn, with a thermometer graphic reading thirty-three degrees.
On-screen text: Source: Treeline Review, Oct 15 | Lows of 33°F to about 54°F | Rated 30°F
Narration: Now, a sleeping bag. Treeline Review's reviewer called the REI Magma 30 accurate to its listed temperature, if a little warm. But her lows ranged from thirty-three to about fifty-four degrees Fahrenheit. The bag is rated thirty. So the rating was never tested at its limit. She also said she would not push it much further below freezing. Treeline is independent, but it runs on an affiliate model.
Claims: [02-c3]

## Scene 5
Visual: Split-screen graphic: "Limit rating" versus "Comfort rating" on a bag label.
On-screen text: Source: Vortex podcast, Down Insulation Explained, Oct 6, at 21:59 and 27:01 | Vortex: Mammoth sponsor
Narration: Here's where it gets complicated. Vortex's podcast hosted Brad Brooks of Argali, a down bag maker. Keep in mind that our selection notes identify Vortex as a Mammoth sponsor. Brooks said no rule requires companies to follow a standard when stating temperature ratings. He said many use the limit rating, and that he considers the comfort rating the honest one. That's one manufacturer's view, but it helps explain why a rating at the limit deserves caution.

## Scene 6
Visual: Side-by-side graphic of two down clusters, one labeled "treated" and one "untreated," with rain on a tent.
On-screen text: Treated down: does the rating hold? | Argali: maker of untreated-down bags | Informal test, not independent data
Narration: Brooks also said treated down can lower a bag's effective rating after a handful of uses. A twenty-degree bag might become a twenty-five. He said his untreated-down bag was fine through almost five days of heavy rain. Argali has a commercial interest in untreated down, and this is his own informal testing and an anecdote. The result of his water submersion test is cut off in our excerpt. Treeline lists the Magma's eight hundred fifty fill goose down as water resistant. But nothing supplied says what coating it uses, or whether its rating changed with use. So these two accounts can't be reconciled. They differ in who is speaking, what was tested, for how long, and why.

## Scene 7
Visual: Hiker on a short, steep loop with a large pack, then a close-up of a rangefinding binocular in light rain.
On-screen text: Source: Rokslide, Sept 23 and Oct 10 | Tract Toric LRF: 2 lbs 5.5 oz | Zeiss Victory RF: 2 lbs 3 oz
Narration: Now two claims that were checked directly. A Rokslide reviewer tested the KUIU Icon 6 pack on short loops of one-point-two miles, with five hundred ten vertical feet gained and lost. He compared it against two competitor frames, and said the Icon 6 completely remedied his heavy-load comfort problem. KUIU says the new frame promises more strength and durability. But the excerpts show no durability test of that frame. The same reviewer judged the Tract Toric rangefinding binocular superior to the much more expensive Zeiss Victory R-F. He reported rain-mode ranging out to just under a thousand yards. Fog limited ranging when targets were mostly obscured. The Tract weighs two pounds five and a half ounces, and the Zeiss two pounds three ounces. He also said he needs more time to judge the Tract's durability.

## Scene 8
Visual: Simple three-column graphic: "Checked directly: held up," "Documented miss: click values," "Untested or disputed: durability, limit ratings, treated down."
On-screen text: Our interpretation, not a source claim
Narration: So what's the pattern? Our take is that claims a reviewer could check directly mostly held up. That means ranging, heavy-load comfort, and listed warmth at moderate temperatures. The claims that matter most over days of hard use were untested, or showed misses. Those are long-term durability, ratings at the limit, treated-down performance, and scope click values. Remember, these are different products from different publishers. This is a pattern, not corroboration of any single claim.

Visual: Plain slide with five short bullet points, appearing one at a time.
On-screen text: Limitations: single reviewers | No Mammoth-condition tests | No lab data | Official page not supplied
Narration: Here are the limits of this evidence. Almost every positive finding is one reviewer's account. Treeline and Rokslide did not test Mammoth conditions, and Treeline is affiliate-funded. It's unclear where the Magma reviewer got the bag's tested range and limit, and she did not retest the rating over time. The click test covers specific older Leupold units, not current models. We have no lab testing, and no evidence from Mammoth organizers or past competitors. Mammoth's official page wasn't supplied, so we leave required status unspecified.
